Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative Executive Summary

—Executive Summary

This proposal reframes local business online presence as a component of city-level digital economic infrastructure, rather than as a website-development programme.

Harar Jugol, the fortified historic town, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2006 and is recognised for its historic urban fabric, African and Islamic cultural traditions, living heritage and handicrafts. UNESCO also notes the continuing challenge of balancing conservation, infrastructure, quality of life and sustainable development.

Ethiopia is undergoing a broader digital transition. The proposal cites World Bank evidence that unique mobile subscribers increased by approximately seven million between 2020 and 2024, and that internet use stood at 22% of the population in 2024.

Core approach

Verified business dataset → digital baseline → targeted improvements → owner/staff training → governed city discovery layer → measurement → scale toolkit.

The original proposal identified a 16-week pilot for approximately 50–100 businesses. This upgraded version expands the structure into a proposed 6–12 month programme with stronger evidence requirements, governance, data protection, KPIs, a logical framework and an explicit baseline stage.

What has changed in this edition

Evidence discipline
Digital gaps treated as hypotheses to be tested at baseline, not as city-wide statistics
Programme length
Expanded from a 16-week delivery to a 12-month phased programme
Governance
Added stakeholder roles, data governance, privacy and security principles
Measurement
Added a KPI framework, logical framework and post-intervention review points
Decision framing
First approval sought is for scoping and baseline, not city-wide delivery

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 1 · Strategic Case

1Strategic Case — Physical City + Digital City

Every city now exists twice: once in physical space, and once in the digital information layer through which people find, evaluate and navigate it.

Physical City

  • Roads and buildings
  • Public spaces and heritage assets
  • Hotels and restaurants
  • Markets and transport
  • Attractions and infrastructure

Digital City

  • Google Maps and search results
  • Business websites
  • Social channels
  • Digital catalogues and reviews
  • Photos, videos, contacts and hours
  • Tourism and destination information

These two layers are complementary. Physical investment creates value; accurate digital information helps people discover, understand, navigate and engage with that value.

Evidence boundary

Better digital information may improve discoverability and reduce information friction, but the economic effect should be tested through baseline and follow-up evaluation rather than assumed.

Why Harar?

Harar is suitable for a pilot because its heritage proposition is highly information-dependent. UNESCO describes Harar Jugol as a rare historic town retaining traditions, urban fabric and Harari Muslim cultural heritage, with recognised handicraft traditions including weaving, basket making and book binding.

These are attributes that visitors actively search for — and that a well-structured digital layer can make legible to them before arrival. The same attributes are poorly served by fragmented, absent or outdated online information.

Positioning statement

Digital visibility is infrastructure, not marketing.

A verified business map, accurate listings and a governed discovery layer function in the same way as a road sign or a public register: they reduce the cost of finding and trusting what already exists.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 2 · Problem Statement

2Problem Statement

The proposal identifies a potential digital visibility gap across markets, guesthouses, restaurants, workshops, retail streets and tourism-facing businesses. These observations are hypotheses to be tested rather than city-wide statistics.

01

Discoverability

Businesses may be absent, duplicated or inaccurately represented on search and mapping platforms.

02

Information quality

Location, phone, opening hours, services, photos or languages may be incomplete.

03

Digital identity

Social accounts may exist without a stable, searchable business identity.

04

Tourism information

Products, experiences, prices or booking and contact pathways may not be clearly explained online.

05

Customer uncertainty

Customers may face difficulty comparing businesses or planning visits before arrival.

06

City data gap

The City Administration may lack a consolidated, verified digital map of the local business ecosystem.

Required evidence

The baseline must establish prevalence, geographic distribution and severity before city-wide targets are fixed. No percentage targets are proposed in this document for that reason.

Why this framing matters

Framing the gap as a hypothesis rather than a fact changes what the City Administration is being asked to approve. Instead of approving an intervention on the assumption that the problem is universal, the first phase is designed to measure the problem — and to make the case for scale on evidence rather than on assertion.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 3 · Four-Perspective Analysis

3Four-Perspective Analysis

The initiative is assessed from four viewpoints. Each perspective carries different expectations and different limits on what digital visibility alone can deliver.

Local businesses

  • Accurate information supports discoverability and credibility.
  • Maps, contacts, services and photos can reduce friction.
  • Consistent digital identity can support partnerships and tourism exposure.

Customers & tourists

  • Visitors need location, hours, services, photos and contacts.
  • Domestic and international visitors may have different language needs.
  • Accurate information can reduce uncertainty without guaranteeing a purchase.

Government

  • A verified business map can complement planning and tourism promotion.
  • A structured dataset provides a monitoring baseline.
  • It must not replace registration, licensing or statistics systems.

City, region and national image

  • Digital information contributes to a destination's first impression.
  • Accurate representation improves consistency.
  • Digital visibility is one component of tourism competitiveness.

Boundary condition

The programme adds a digital information layer. It does not replace physical development, heritage management, tourism investment, business registration or official government statistics systems — and is not proposed as a substitute for any of them.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 4 · Programme Objective and Results

4Programme Objective and Results

Overall objective

Establish a measurable, scalable digital business-presence system

Connect Harar's physical economic and tourism assets with accurate, discoverable and maintainable digital information.

Specific Objectives

  1. Create a verified digital business register for the pilot area.
  2. Measure the baseline digital presence of participating businesses.
  3. Correct or improve core discovery information — name, location, contact, hours, category and photographs.
  4. Provide targeted websites, catalogues, social setup, local SEO and communication improvements where justified by the audit.
  5. Train owners and staff in digital management and online safety.
  6. Create a governed city-level discovery layer built from verified participating data.
  7. Establish a dashboard and repeatable toolkit for expansion to additional districts.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 5 · Programme Architecture (1 of 2)

5Proposed Programme Architecture

The programme is structured in seven phases across twelve months. Phases overlap deliberately: mapping, auditing and improvement work are sequenced so that findings from one phase inform the next.

Phase 1 Month 1

Stakeholder Mobilisation & Baseline Design

Confirm pilot district or cluster, governance arrangements, data fields, verification procedure, consent approach and methodology; map existing official datasets to avoid duplication.

Phase 2 Months 2–3

Business Mapping

Collect identity, category, location, contacts, links, maps, hours, photos, languages, products and services, and tourism relevance for each business in the pilot area.

Phase 3 Months 2–4

Digital Presence Audit

Assess discoverability, accuracy, maps presence, mobile usability, social activity, reviews, contactability, visual quality, multilingual access and tourism information against a structured scorecard.

Phase 4 Months 4–6

Improvement Packages

Delivered selectively, based on audit findings: map and profile correction; local SEO; website or storefront; catalogue; social setup; booking and contact workflow; photography; and skills or maintenance support.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 5 · Programme Architecture (2 of 2)
Phase 5 Months 6–9

City Digital Discovery Layer

A public directory or destination layer built from verified participating data. This is explicitly a discovery service, not a business registration database, and does not replace official records.

Phase 6 Months 6–9

Training & Handover

Practical training delivered in Amharic, Afaan Oromo and/or Harari as appropriate, covering profile management, reviews, publishing, security and correction procedures — with written guides handed over to participants.

Phase 7 Months 9–12

Monitoring & Scale-Up

Baseline, implementation and post-intervention measurement, with review points at 3, 6 and 12 months where feasible, and a documented scale toolkit for additional districts.

Sequencing note

Phases 2 and 3 run in parallel so that mapping data feeds the audit without a second field visit. Phase 4 improvement work begins only after audit findings are validated and prioritised, so that effort is directed by evidence rather than by assumption.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 6 · Business Data Model

6Business Data Model

The register is the foundation of the entire programme. Without a consistent, verified and maintainable data model, later phases cannot be measured or scaled.

Identity

Business ID, name, category, subcategory.

Location

Address, coordinates, kebele, sub-city or district.

Contacts

Phone, WhatsApp, email, website.

Platforms

Google Maps and profile, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok.

Offer

Hours, products and services, languages, tourism relevance.

Verification

Photos and date, reviews, verification status and date, intervention and follow-up.

Data Separation Principle

Data collected under this programme must be separated into three categories, each with different handling rules:

  • Public business information — information a business is willing to publish, and which may appear in listings or the discovery layer.
  • Operational project information — internal delivery, verification and follow-up records used to manage the programme.
  • Personal data — information relating to identifiable individuals, requiring additional protection and stricter handling.

Design rule

Every data field must have a stated purpose, a defined source, a verification date and a retention rule before collection begins. Fields without a purpose are not collected.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 6.2 · Digital Presence Scorecard

6.2Digital Presence Scorecard

Each business is assessed against ten dimensions. The scorecard produces a comparable baseline and makes improvement measurable at follow-up.

Dimension Example evidence Measurement principle
DiscoverabilitySearch and map findabilityDefined search protocol and sample
AccuracyName, phone, address, hoursVerified against the business
Website & mobileAvailability and usabilityStructured checklist
Maps presenceListing existence and correctnessPlatform audit
SocialActivity and identityDefined observation window
ReviewsPresence, recency, response practiceDescriptive metrics; no ranking
ContactabilityPhone, message, emailApproved test protocol
Products & servicesClarity of offer informationChecklist
Visual presentationCurrent photos, brand consistencyChecklist
Multilingual accessRelevant language informationDefined criteria

The scorecard is descriptive rather than competitive: businesses are not ranked against one another, and review counts are not treated as a quality measure.

Measurement integrity

The same scorecard, protocol and observation window are applied at baseline and at follow-up. Where an element cannot be measured consistently, it is recorded as a qualitative note rather than converted into a number.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 8.2 · Data Governance, Privacy & Security

8.2Data Governance, Privacy & Security

Because the programme collects business and personal information at scale, data governance is treated as a core deliverable rather than an administrative afterthought.

  • Collect only data that is necessary for a stated purpose.
  • Use clear consent or authorisation where required.
  • Distinguish public business information from private personal information.
  • Record the source of every data field and its verification date.
  • Provide a documented correction and removal process for businesses.
  • Do not republish restricted information without authorisation.
  • Use role-based access, secure credential handling and regular backups.
  • Do not publish sensitive personal information under any circumstances.
  • Define retention and deletion rules before collection begins.
  • Obtain legal and administrative review for applicable Ethiopian requirements.

Accountability

A named data owner, a written procedure, a correction channel.

Before Phase 2 begins, the programme must identify who holds the register, who may access it, how corrections are processed and how the register is maintained after handover.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 9.2 · Logical Framework

9.2Logical Framework

The logical framework connects the programme's intended impact to its outputs and states how each level is verified.

Level Statement Indicator and verification
Impact Improved digital discoverability of Harar's local economic and tourism ecosystem Baseline-to-follow-up change; audit and analytics where available
Outcome 1 Businesses have more accurate and usable digital information Percentage meeting minimum standard; digital audit
Outcome 2 City stakeholders have a structured business visibility dataset Verified register plus documented update process
Outcome 3 Businesses gain practical digital-management capability Training completion plus capability survey
Outputs Register and baseline, priority improvements, discovery layer and scale toolkit Project database, delivery records, platform review, handover package

Interpretation rule

Outputs are counted directly. Outcomes are measured through before-and-after comparison. Impact is treated as a directional finding, not a proven causal claim, unless a comparable control group is available.

Harar City Digital Visibility Initiative 12 · Pilot Design & Field Tools

12Pilot Design & Field Tools

The planning range is approximately 50–100 businesses for one pilot district, with final selection conditional on baseline results and operational capacity.

  1. Select a commercially and/or tourism-relevant cluster with manageable boundaries.
  2. Include different business types across the cluster.
  3. Document participation and non-participation.
  4. Measure the same indicators before and after intervention.
  5. Include owner and customer feedback where feasible.
  6. Use results to determine whether expansion is justified.

Data Collection Template

Digital Business Audit Checklist

  • Can the business be found by name?
  • Is the map location accurate?
  • Are phone and contact details correct?
  • Are opening hours present and current?
  • Is the category appropriate?
  • Are photos current and useful?
  • Is there a website or catalogue where needed?
  • Does the website work on mobile?
  • Are products and services understandable?
  • Are social accounts identifiable and active?
  • Are reviews visible and is there a response process?
  • Are relevant languages represented?
  • Can a visitor understand how to contact, visit or book?
  • Does the digital presentation accurately represent the physical business?

13Decision Requested

Approve the scoping and baseline phase

The first decision is not to approve a city-wide website programme.

It is to approve a structured scoping and baseline phase that measures the current situation, identifies priority businesses and confirms governance, technical, financial and maintenance requirements for a scalable pilot.

01

Scope

Confirm stakeholders, geography and governance.

02

Measure

Build the verified business dataset and baseline.

03

Implement

Deliver targeted improvements and training.

04

Evaluate

Measure post-intervention change.

05

Document

Create the scale toolkit and maintenance model.

06

Scale

Use evidence to determine whether and how to expand.

14Conclusion

Modernising a city is not only about how the city looks physically. It is also about how easily people can discover, understand, navigate and engage with its businesses, services and cultural assets digitally.

The programme does not replace physical development, heritage management, tourism investment or official government systems. It adds a practical digital layer that can connect those investments to the people who need to discover and use them.

Build the city physically.
Make it discoverable digitally.
